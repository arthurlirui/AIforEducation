\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{Canvas3D}, a four-module framework that transforms static
oil paintings and ink-wash artworks into 3D-aware, artistically controllable
animated videos. Our key design insight is the \emph{modular separation of
perceptual, geometric, compositional, and temporal concerns} across four
specialized modules, each of which can be optimized and upgraded independently
and which communicate only through small, interpretable handshake
representations.

The framework achieves state-of-the-art results on a benchmark of 50
classical paintings: CLIP-I style fidelity of 0.86, LPIPS perceptual
distance of 0.28, disocclusion PSNR of 29.1\,dB, and FID-VID temporal
coherence of 18.3. User studies confirm a strong preference for
Canvas3D outputs across artistic authenticity, motion naturalness, and
compositional-intent alignment, and ablations validate the contribution
of every module, with the largest degradations occurring when the
depth-conditioned inpainting module or the dual control branches are
removed.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
Several limitations suggest directions for future work:
(i)~the per-frame conditioning in ArtiFlow operates on 2D projections,
so extreme viewpoint changes (beyond roughly $60^\circ$) can introduce
hallucinated textures inconsistent with the underlying 3D model;
(ii)~the feed-forward 3D predictor's reconstruction quality depends on
the object's visibility in the original painting---heavily occluded
regions yield degraded geometry;
(iii)~the disocclusion inpainting module assumes locally coherent
background texture, which may fail for large gaps in sparse
compositions;
(iv)~objects are processed serially, and parallelized scheduling of
the 3D lifting calls would reduce end-to-end latency.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Future work.}
We plan to explore: (a)~multi-canvas scene fusion, enabling composition
from objects extracted from different paintings; (b)~radiance-field
representations for the background layer, which would allow more
flexible camera trajectories; (c)~interactive real-time line-art
composition with progressive 3D rendering; and (d)~audio-visual
alignment for narratively-scored animation generation.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Ethics statement.}
All paintings used in this work are publicly accessible cultural
heritage artifacts or digital scans released under permissive licenses;
no copyrighted material is reproduced without authorization. All
generated videos are explicitly labeled as AI-generated content.
Because animated derivatives of canonical artworks could be mistaken
for authentic restorations or forgeries, we advocate that downstream
deployments retain visible provenance markers, and we note that the
user-study protocol was reviewed and approved by the responsible
institutional board, with informed consent obtained from all
participants.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Reproducibility statement.}
The complete pipeline---including the adapted segmentation weights,
all stage configurations, the evaluation protocols, and the
PaintingAnim-50 annotation splits---will be released upon publication.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:canvas3d} specifies the full execution order and
all hyperparameters ($\lambda_c$, $s_d$, $s_e$, $\tau_{\mathrm{mo}}$,
$T$, $\sigma_{\max}$) are reported in the main text, enabling exact
reimplementation of every reported result.
