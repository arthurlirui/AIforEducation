\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{Canvas3D-v2}, a seven-module framework that transforms
static oil paintings and ink-wash artworks into 3D-aware,
viewpoint-controllable, and spatial-relation-anchored animated videos.
Our key design insight is the \emph{modular separation of perceptual,
geometric, compositional, relational, viewpoint, and temporal concerns}
across seven specialized modules, each of which can be optimized and
upgraded independently and which communicate only through small,
interpretable handshake representations.

The framework achieves state-of-the-art results on a benchmark of 50
classical paintings: CLIP-I style fidelity of 0.86, LPIPS perceptual
quality of 0.28, disocclusion PSNR of 29.1\,dB, FID-VID temporal
coherence of 18.3, Novel-View IoU of 0.78 across five viewpoint modes,
and spatial-relation accuracy of 0.88 under user-edited graph
constraints. User studies confirm strong preference for Canvas3D-v2
outputs across artistic authenticity, motion naturalness,
compositional-intent alignment, and viewpoint-exploration quality.
Ablations validate each module's contribution, with the largest
degradations occurring when the depth-aware inpainting module, the
edge control branch, the SceneGraph3D relation signal, or the
SpatialFlow verification loop is removed.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Limitations.}
Several limitations suggest directions for future work:
(i)~the per-frame conditioning operates at the 2D projection level, so
extreme viewpoint changes (>$60^\circ$ rotation) introduce hallucinated
textures inconsistent with the underlying 3D model;
(ii)~the feed-forward 3D predictor's single-image reconstruction quality
depends on the object's visibility in the original painting---heavily
occluded regions produce degraded 3D geometry;
(iii)~our color-inpainting module assumes locally similar textures,
which may fail for large disocclusion regions in sparse compositions;
(iv)~the GNN relation encoder uses a fixed two-layer mean-aggregation
architecture; richer attention-based graph encoders may capture
higher-order relational structure;
(v)~the relation-consistency verification loop currently re-prompts at
most once per segment; iterative refinement with learned stopping
criteria may improve recovery on complex edits.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Future work.}
We plan to explore: (a)~multi-canvas scene fusion, enabling composition
from objects extracted from different paintings, with cross-painting
relation normalization in SceneGraph3D; (b)~neural radiance
field representations for the background layer, which would allow more
flexible camera trajectories in ViewCtrl; (c)~interactive real-time
line-art and scene-graph composition with progressive 3D rendering;
(d)~audio-visual alignment for narratively-scored animation generation;
and (e)~learning the GNN relation encoder end-to-end with the video
diffusion backbone, so that relational context is optimized for
generation quality rather than fixed a priori.
