\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We review prior work along the four threads that motivate our
decomposition and position Canvas3D at their intersection.

\subsection{From Pixel-Level Stylization to Painting Animation}

Early painting animation treats the input as a pixel grid to be
stylized over time. Static style
transfer~\cite{gatys2016image,johnson2016perceptual,huang2017arbitrary,park2019arbitrary,deng2021stytr2}
established that artistic appearance can be re-injected into arbitrary
content; its video extensions addressed temporal coherence via
optical-flow regularization~\cite{ruder2016artistic,chen2017coherent,huang2017realtime,wang2020consistent,deng2021arbitrary}
and content deformation fields~\cite{ouyang2024codef}. A second lineage
synthesizes the painting \emph{process} itself, as stroke-level
time-lapses~\cite{zhao2020painting,zou2021stylized,liu2021neuralstrokes,madono2023data},
but reconstructs how a painting was made rather than how its subjects
could move; a narrower thread animates painted
faces~\cite{guo2024liveportrait,tan2024edtalk} only. Diffusion-era
methods---inversion-based
stylization~\cite{zhang2023inversion,chen2024artadapter}, video
editing~\cite{ceylan2023pix2video,chai2023stablevideo,geyer2024tokenflow,liang2024flowvid},
and recent video stylizers~\cite{ye2025stylemaster,li2026echostyle,jones2026refvfx}---preserve
appearance under text-driven edits but provide no geometric handle on
object pose. Domain-specific systems for Chinese ink
painting~\cite{liang2021instance,he2023bitgan,liu2026chipdiff,zhang2025inkspirit}
follow the same pattern. Across all of these lines, appearance is
preserved while spatial structure is not: no method offers
object-level, 3D-grounded motion control.

\subsection{3D Scene Representations and Single-Image Lifting}

Scene representations evolved from implicit radiance
fields~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf} to explicit primitives: 3D Gaussian
Splatting~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} renders anisotropic Gaussians in real
time, and 2D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{huang20242dgs} improves surface
accuracy. More relevant to us is the shift from per-scene optimization
to feed-forward prediction: DreamGaussian~\cite{tang2024dreamgaussian}
generates 3D assets with generative priors, while
pixelSplat~\cite{charatan2024pixelsplat}, AnySplat~\cite{jiang2025anysplat},
and TripoSplat~\cite{tripo2025tritosplat} regress Gaussian parameters
directly from one or few images. These predictors make it feasible to
lift individual painting regions into 3D, but none addresses the
composition of multiple lifted objects into a coherent, artistically
controllable scene.

\subsection{Sketch and Line Art as a Composition Interface}

Sketches have long served as an interface for shape
prototyping~\cite{olson2009sketch}. Recent systems use them for
generative ends: ShadowDraw~\cite{luo2025shadowdraw} synthesizes
compositional shadow art, LineArt~\cite{wang2025lineart} transfers
appearance onto design drawings, and region-aware
simplification~\cite{nguyen2024region} and volumetric animation
toolkits~\cite{foxgieg2024lightning} demonstrate the expressive range
of hand-drawn control. In diffusion pipelines, sketches typically act
as appearance or boundary conditions, as in ControlNet-style edge
injection~\cite{zhang2023controlnet}. Canvas3D instead treats line art
as a \emph{spatial constraint}: the sketch is a target silhouette to
which a 3D object is geometrically registered, resolving the
which-object-where problem in scene assembly.

\subsection{Controllable Image-to-Video Generation}

Diffusion transformers~\cite{peebles2023dit} and video diffusion
models~\cite{ho2022vdm,ho2022video} underpin a family of scalable I2V
backbones~\cite{blattmann2023svd,wan2025,guo2023animatediff,yang2025cogvideox}.
Controllability is usually pursued by auxiliary conditioning:
ControlNet~\cite{zhang2023controlnet} injects edge, depth, and pose
maps, with video extensions~\cite{wang2025easycontrol} and efficient
variants~\cite{peng2024controlnext}; cinematic shot
design~\cite{xing2025motioncanvas} and clipart
animation~\cite{wu2025aniclipart} achieve compelling results without
explicit 3D supervision. All of these condition \emph{per frame}: they
cannot reason about object identity, depth ordering, or occlusion
across frames, and drift under viewpoint change. Canvas3D grounds its
control signals in an explicit, object-level 3D scene that is
composited and rendered before synthesis.

\subsection{Painting-Specific Animation Pipelines}

A small body of work targets paintings directly. Domain-adapted
detectors~\cite{chen2025pldsyolo} and art
datasets~\cite{saleh2016metric} show that natural-image
backbones~\cite{jocher2023yolov8,wang2024yolov9,wang2024yolov10,tian2025yolov12}
and segmentation foundation
models~\cite{kirillov2023sam,ravi2024sam2} require art-specific
fine-tuning, due to brushstroke texture and intentional boundary
blending. Closest to our setting, ``Printing the
Underdetermined''~\cite{ming2026printing} translates paintings to 3D
via video generative models but processes the image as a whole, and
OilPaintingRevive~\cite{oilpaintingrevive2026} combines 2DGS with
video refinement yet relies on per-scene skeleton binding that limits
compositional flexibility. Canvas3D differs on four counts:
object-level rather than image-level manipulation, line art as the
composition interface, depth-aware inpainting for disocclusion, and
multi-modal geometric conditioning for video synthesis.
