\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Classical paintings---oil on canvas, ink on silk, pigment on
fresco---depict motion, narrative, and spatial depth that their static
medium can only imply~\cite{lowenthal2015heritage}. Animating them as
\emph{explorable} videos, in which the viewpoint can move and painted
subjects can be repositioned, has therefore become an active goal at
the intersection of computer vision, generative modeling, and digital
cultural heritage~\cite{dong2024enhancingpainting}.

Progress on this goal, however, is split along two lines that do not
meet. Image-to-video (I2V) diffusion
models~\cite{blattmann2023svd,wan2025,guo2023animatediff,yang2025cogvideox},
together with conditioning mechanisms such as
ControlNet~\cite{zhang2023controlnet,wang2025easycontrol,peng2024controlnext},
animate a single image into a short clip with plausible per-frame
dynamics. Applied to paintings, they exhibit two shortcomings: they
operate purely in 2D pixel space, with no notion of depth ordering,
self-occlusion, or spatial layout, so they can neither synthesize novel
viewpoints nor keep 3D structure consistent across frames; and temporal
coherence degrades quickly beyond a few seconds, since latent
interpolation has no geometric anchor for the motion trajectory.
Reconstruction methods~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf,kerbl20233dgs,huang20242dgs}
provide the missing geometry, and feed-forward monocular
variants~\cite{tripo2025tritosplat,tang2024dreamgaussian,charatan2024pixelsplat}
can lift a single image to 3D, but they are optimized for photorealistic
fidelity: applied to paintings, they replace brushstrokes with geometric
artifacts, flatten impasto texture, and blur color boundaries under
novel-view projection.

The underlying difficulty is that \emph{3D structure and artistic style}
favor conflicting representations, and neither model family achieves
both. The conflict is most acute when the task demands
\textbf{object-level manipulation}: moving a painted figure to a new
compositional position while preserving its painterly identity and
plausibly filling the disoccluded background.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Our approach.}
Rather than training one network to jointly optimize style and
structure---an ill-posed objective whose gradients pull in opposite
directions---we factor the problem into four sub-problems, each
addressed by a specialist and connected to the next by a small,
interpretable intermediate representation (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}):

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{PaintParse} performs perceptual grouping: it
    decomposes the painting into foreground objects and a background
    layer with an art-domain-adapted segmentation backbone, and orders
    the layers by monocular depth.
    \item \textbf{MonoSplat} handles geometry: it lifts each foreground
    region into a 3D Gaussian representation via feed-forward
    single-image prediction, rescaled to metric units by the PaintParse
    depth cue, and assembles an object-level library.
    \item \textbf{SketchComp} encodes compositional intent: it accepts
    hand-drawn line art specifying target positions, registers each 3D
    object to its sketched contour by a rigid transformation, and fills
    disocclusion gaps with depth-conditioned inpainting.
    \item \textbf{ArtiFlow} renders the composited scene into
    multi-view keyframes and drives a video diffusion backbone with
    dual control branches (depth and edge), so that object motion
    follows explicit 3D geometry rather than learned heuristics.
\end{itemize}

Each intermediate representation carries exactly what the next stage
needs, which localizes errors instead of compounding them. The result
is a system supporting object repositioning, multi-view exploration,
and style-preserving animation---capabilities that no prior
single-modality pipeline offers.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A modular factorization of painting-to-video generation into
    four specialists---PaintParse, MonoSplat, SketchComp, and
    ArtiFlow---that communicate through small, interpretable
    intermediate representations; to our knowledge, this is the first
    end-to-end pipeline for 3D-aware, artistically controllable
    painting animation.
    \item An object-level 3D lifting pipeline (MonoSplat) that couples
    art-domain-adapted segmentation with feed-forward monocular 3D
    prediction and depth-derived metric rescaling
    (Eq.~\ref{eq:metric_scale}), yielding a reusable library of
    metric-consistent 3D objects from a single painting.
    \item A sketch-conditioned composition algorithm (SketchComp)
    whose contour-based rigid registration jointly optimizes region IoU
    and boundary Chamfer distance (Eq.~\ref{eq:contour_reg}), combined
    with a depth-conditioned inpainting module that fills disocclusion
    gaps (Eq.~\ref{eq:gap}) while preserving background texture.
    \item A geometry-grounded video synthesis workflow (ArtiFlow)
    whose dual control branches (Eq.~\ref{eq:dual_control}) and
    first-frame latent anchoring bind temporal motion to explicit 3D
    structure with per-object control.
    \item A comprehensive evaluation on a benchmark of 50 classical
    paintings, showing state-of-the-art style fidelity, perceptual
    quality, and temporal coherence, with user studies confirming
    preference for artistic authenticity, motion naturalness, and
    compositional-intent alignment.
\end{enumerate}
