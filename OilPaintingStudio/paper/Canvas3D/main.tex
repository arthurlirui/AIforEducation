\begin{abstract}
Animating static artworks---oil paintings, ink-wash scrolls, and
frescoes---into explorable, narratively coherent videos is a long-standing
goal at the intersection of computer vision, generative modeling, and digital
cultural heritage. Existing approaches remain bifurcated: image-to-video
diffusion models produce visually fluent motion but collapse three-dimensional
structure under viewpoint change, while feed-forward 3D reconstruction
pipelines capture geometry at the cost of obliterating painterly texture,
impasto, and brushstroke identity. The root cause is a fundamental
\emph{objective tension}: artistic style fidelity and 3D scene structure are
governed by incompatible optimization targets, and no single end-to-end model
satisfies both, especially when the task demands object-level manipulation.
We propose \textbf{Canvas3D-v2}, a framework that resolves this tension through
a \emph{modular decomposition of concerns}. Rather than training a monolithic
network, Canvas3D-v2 factors the painting-to-video problem into seven
independently optimized, mutually reinforcing sub-problems:
\emph{(i) perceptual grouping} of a painting into semantically meaningful
foreground objects and a preserved background, jointly with monocular
depth-derived layer ordering; \emph{(ii) per-object geometric lifting} that
converts each 2D region into a metric-scaled 3D representation and assembles
a reusable, retrievable object-level model library; \emph{(iii) line-art
scene composition} in which hand-drawn sketches serve as \emph{spatial
constraints} rather than stylistic hints, solved by a contour-based rigid
registration that places each 3D object at its sketched location and a
depth-conditioned inpainting module that restores disoccluded background
texture in a geometry-aware manner; \emph{(iv) 3D scene graph construction}
that organizes the lifted objects and their pairwise spatial
relations---topological, depth-ordering, and physical-support---into an
explicit, editable, serializable graph representation, providing a structured
intermediate that subsequent viewpoint and video stages can reason over;
\emph{(v) viewpoint-controllable novel-view rendering} that accepts a user
camera trajectory specification (manual, orbit, dolly, crane, or
auto-orbit) and renders multi-view keyframes with per-node visibility masks,
enabling arbitrary viewpoint exploration rather than a fixed arc; and
\emph{(vi) spatial-relation-driven video synthesis} that injects a third
control signal---a graph-neural-network-encoded relation embedding pooled
per-frame over the visible node set---into the video diffusion backbone
alongside depth and edge, anchoring temporal motion to explicit 3D
\emph{spatial relations} rather than learned heuristics, with a
relation-consistency verification loop that re-prompts when generated frames
violate the edited scene graph. The key insight is that \emph{separating
perceptual, geometric, compositional, relational, viewpoint, and temporal
concerns} turns an ill-posed, conflicting joint problem into seven
well-posed sub-problems, each addressable by an off-the-shelf specialist,
while their interaction yields capabilities---arbitrary object repositioning,
viewpoint-controllable exploration, and spatial-relation-anchored
animation---that no single-modality pipeline offers. On a curated benchmark
of classical paintings, Canvas3D-v2 achieves state-of-the-art style fidelity,
perceptual quality, temporal coherence, novel-view consistency, and
spatial-relation accuracy, with user studies confirming strong preference
for artistic authenticity, motion naturalness, compositional-intent
alignment, and viewpoint-exploration quality.
\end{abstract}
