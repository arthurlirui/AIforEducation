\begin{abstract}
We present \emph{Depth-as-Radius}, a non-photorealistic rendering
pipeline that turns a single RGB image into a Yayoi
Kusama--style polka dot composition in which dot size, rather than
luminance, encodes scene depth. A monocular depth estimate is
normalized over the foreground region and passed through a power-law
falloff with multiplicative Gaussian jitter, so that near surfaces
receive large dots while distant content dissolves into fine specks.
Semantic segmentation from the same backbone (YOLO26) assigns distinct
dot colors to foreground and background, producing a figure--ground
separation characteristic of Kusama's installations. Because the
required radius range (roughly 1--15\,px) exceeds what a single
stipple layer can support without merging, the renderer decomposes
the output into three layers---fine stipple, depth-emphasis, and
edge/background---unified by a cross-layer overlap-pruning step.
Experiments on three natural images and five palettes show
controllable depth encoding (radius span 0.5--15.4\,px within one
composition), preserved dot separation (maximum blob ratio below
0.65\% of the canvas), and interpretable artistic control. Pretrained
monocular vision models thus supply geometric and semantic control
signals that purely luminance-based stippling cannot express.
\end{abstract}
