\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Stippling and Point-Based Rendering}

Computational stippling began with the floating-points method of
\citet{deussen2000floating}, which distributes points according to a
density field via Lloyd relaxation \citep{lloyd1982least}.
\citet{secord2002weighted} refined this into Weighted Voronoi
Stippling, combining rejection sampling with density-weighted
centroids to achieve blue-noise distributions; it remains the standard
and forms our algorithmic foundation. Subsequent work addressed
capacity constraints \citep{balzer2009capacity}, real-time blue noise
\citep{kopf2006recursive}, example-based tone texture
\citep{kim2009stippling,kim2008feature}, and perceptual quality
assessment \citep{spicker2017quantifying}; \citet{martin2017survey}
survey the field. Poisson disk sampling
\citep{bridson2007fast,ulichney1988dithering} offers an alternative
point process but does not directly support density modulation.

All of these methods map dot size and density to one scalar
field---luminance. To our knowledge, no prior stippling method uses
scene depth as a control dimension for dot size. We treat depth as a
first-class artistic variable alongside luminance.

\subsection{Monocular Depth Estimation}

Deep networks have made single-image depth estimation practical since
\citet{eigen2014depth}, with successive improvements from residual
architectures \citep{laina2016deeper}, ordinal regression
\citep{fu2018deep}, and transformer backbones
\citep{ranftl2021vision}. Robust zero-shot models such as MiDaS
\citep{ranftl2022towards} and Depth Anything \citep{yang2024depth}
generalize across domains; the YOLO26 family
\citep{ultralytics2025yolo26} additionally couples dense prediction
with real-time semantic segmentation. In NPR, depth maps have mainly
served view-dependent effects such as depth of field
\citep{winnemoller2006real}; modulating the size of individual
rendering primitives by depth appears unexplored.

\subsection{Neural Style Transfer and Diffusion Stylization}

Neural style transfer \citep{gatys2015neural} recombines content and
style through Gram-matrix optimization; feed-forward variants
\citep{johnson2016perceptual,ulyanov2016instance} made it fast.
Diffusion models \citep{rombach2022high} with conditional control
\citep{zhang2023adding} and low-rank fine-tuning \citep{hu2022lora}
can imitate a named artist's style from text prompts alone---a
Kusama-style LoRA combined with a depth-conditioned ControlNet is the
obvious learned alternative to our approach. These methods, however,
offer no explicit control over individual primitives: dots are
synthesized as texture, not placed by rule. Our pipeline is
complementary---every dot's position, size, and color follows an
interpretable rule, and the output can itself serve as a conditioning
image for diffusion refinement.

\subsection{Art-Style Rendering}

Procedural and physics-based models exist for impressionism
\citep{litwinowicz1997processing,hertzmann1998painterly}, watercolor
\citep{curtis1997computer}, and other media. Kusama's polka dot art,
despite its iconic status, has received little computational
attention. We contribute a domain-specific framework that encodes its
four signature traits---hard-edged dots, multi-scale coexistence,
high-chroma palettes, and horror vacui---as algorithmic parameters.
